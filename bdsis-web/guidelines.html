BDSIS Brand Guidelines · Web · Version 1.0 · August 2026

Designing the BDSIS website

BDSIS sits inside the University, so its web pages inherit PolyU's institutional furniture: the banner, the red, the grey and Helvetica. To that they add two things of their own, a warm cream ground and a kit of shapes drawn from the programme artwork. This document specifies both, and every rule in it describes web.css, the stylesheet that the site itself runs on.

01

Principles

Four rules settle most questions.

University furniture is not ours to redesign. Inherit it, do not restyle it.

Helvetica does the talking. No second display face. Hierarchy comes from size, weight and space.

One red. #A02337, PMS 194 C, banner block through to buttons and shape markers. A second programme red cost a plate and a rule and bought nothing.

Draw with the shape kit. Four primitives, one per family. No rounded rectangles, no shadows on shapes, no outlines.

02

The logo

Fixed artwork. Never redrawn, respaced or recoloured. The shape kit in §08 is its geometry, the families in §07 its palette.

Two lockups

Standalone. Shown as it is normally used: beside the University logo, at 90% of its height. Also for the site banner, internal documents and campus signage, wherever the PolyU context is already established.
Endorsed. Carries “PolyU” in the wordmark, so it never sits beside the University logo. For recruitment, partnerships, conferences and anything a stranger sees first.

Full colour and monocolor

Full colour · the primary form. Use it wherever colour is available.
Monocolor · one-colour print, engraving, embroidery, fax. The mark keeps its full geometry, so the quadrants read as shape instead of colour.

Tangerine is the one family that needs two plates

Ambers and vermilion sit 40° apart, so no single Tangerine ink reaches both. Magenta overprinted on the amber does.

A full-colour spot job is four plates: 194 C, 1375 C, 5777 C, 2222 C, plus the magenta and black already on press. One is the University's own corporate ink.

For a one-colour job use the monocolor lockup. It is the only approved single-ink version. Never build a reduced-colour approximation of the full-colour mark.

Never redraw these gradients by eye. Place the supplied vector: 117° appears three times, and rotating one breaks the relationship between quadrants.

On dark grounds

Dark · the mark keeps its colour, the wordmark inverts. The mark is legible on the dark ground as drawn and must not be knocked out.
Dark Mono Gold · the monocolor lockup in #F0D08A. Ceremonial material only, covered in §14.

The wordmark is a mask, not baked artwork. One file per lockup serves black, white and gold. Set color on .logo and everything inherits.

Combined with the University logo

Both marks sit on the same ground, in the same colourway. Two files ship, polyu-logo-colour.svg and polyu-logo-white.svg. Never a filtered copy of the other.

On a light ground · University logo in full colour, programme logo in full colour.
Reversed · both logos in white. Use the monocolor lockup, not the full-colour mark.
Don't

Two grounds and two colourways. The University logo reversed on red, beside a full-colour programme logo on white, reads as a seam, not a lockup.

Don't

Wrong order. The University logo always leads.

When both appear use the standalone lockup, University logo leading. Separate with a hairline or a gap of at least the mark's height.

Side by side, the mark is 90% of the University logo's box height: 32 px gives 29, 7.2 mm gives 6.5. Too narrow for both? Use the endorsed lockup.

The mark alone

The mark may stand alone only where the programme name already appears in the same field of view. A favicon, a social avatar, an app icon, or a document footer whose header carries the full logo.

It is built from quarter circles and a diagonally split square on a 3×3 grid. That construction is the source of the shape kit.

Clear space

Clear space is half the mark's height on all four sides. Nothing enters it: not type, not another logo, not an image edge, not a page trim.

½ × mark height
The hatched band is the exclusion zone. It scales with the logo, since it is a ratio and not a fixed measurement.

The logo in print

Print asks what the ink sits on, how few colours are available, and how small the mark can go before a press closes it up.

Do

Full colour on a warm uncoated stock. The mark's gradients hold on uncoated paper because the families are mid-tone.

Don't

Full colour on a mid-tone or coloured stock. The mark loses separation and the wordmark loses contrast.

Do

One-colour jobs use the monocolor lockup, where the quadrant gaps carry the construction.

Don't

Never desaturate the full-colour mark to make a one-colour version. The quadrants collapse to near-identical greys.

Do

On a family field such as a booth wall, a roll-up or a folder, reverse the monocolor lockup out in white.

Don't

Never box the logo in a white patch to force it onto a coloured ground.

Do

Vector. Edges stay clean at any size, the gradients stay smooth, and a printer can trap it.

Don't

The same logo from a low-resolution PNG. Edges step and the gradients band into flat strips.

Always supply vector. A rasterised copy bands on press and cannot be trapped. If a supplier asks for a PNG, ask what they intend to do with it.

Never

Each of these is the real logo, rendered wrong.

Never recolour
The mark is never tinted into one family colour, or into PolyU Red. Use the monocolor lockup in ink, white or gold.
Never stretch
No stretching, squashing, rotation or skew. Scale proportionally only.
Never respace
The gap is 0.266 × the mark height. Opening or closing it breaks the lockup.
Never add effects
No shadow, outline, glow, bevel or container box.
Never set in live type

Never on a busy or family ground
On a family field or a photograph, contrast is not guaranteed. Use the monocolor lockup in white.

Nothing enters the exclusion zone. Not a rule, not a strapline, not another mark.
Never double the endorsement

06

Typography

Helvetica is PolyU's official English typeface, Helvetica Neue the specified cut. MHei is the Chinese face. Roboto and Heiti are the sanctioned online fallbacks.

--font

"Helvetica Neue", "HelveticaNeue", Helvetica, Roboto, Arial,
"MHei", "Heiti TC", "PingFang HK", "Microsoft JhengHei", sans-serif;

Read this before you sign off a design

Helvetica Neue is a system font on Apple devices, not a licensed webfont. Roughly half your visitors, on Windows and Android, will see Roboto or Arial instead. Both are narrower than Helvetica Neue at the same size, so a headline set to fill a line on a Mac will break early or leave a gap elsewhere.

Two consequences, both mandatory. Check every headline in Arial before it ships, and never set type so tight only Helvetica Neue survives it.

Tracking

Helvetica Neue sets loose at display sizes and tight at small ones, so tracking is negative at the top of the scale and positive at the bottom.

  • Above 32 px, pull in to --tr-display (−0.024em) or --tr-heading (−0.015em).
  • 15–20 px, leave at zero. Helvetica is already correct here.
  • Uppercase below 14 px, open up to --tr-label (+0.12em). Uppercase Helvetica at 12 px is unreadable without it.

Chinese

MHei is the Chinese face, Heiti the fallback. Latin first in the stack, so mixed sentences keep Helvetica for the Latin.

  • Set line height to 1.8 (--lh-loose), because CJK glyphs fill the em box and crowd at 1.65.
  • Set tracking to 0, including in headings. Negative tracking collides Chinese characters.

The one-corner rule

A container is square-cornered, or carries a single 28 px quarter-round on the bottom-right. Never two, never four.

Fully square. Used for dense grids and tables.

Cell tiles

Where a photograph is unavailable, use a cell tile: a family field carrying one oversized shape. Never a stock photograph.

Rotate the motif down a grid through .cell-tile and its modifiers, so a column never repeats itself.

Signature fields

The kit used generatively. Any text becomes a field of cells that belongs to that text and nothing else: a name, a student number, a cohort. It is the one part of the system that produces artwork nobody drew, and the same text always produces the same field, on any machine, forever.

Used across the reverse of the business card, the ID card and the luggage tag, where it does the work a photograph would otherwise do. Not decoration and not a pattern library. A field stands for a person.

It is a fingerprint, not a message. Nothing can be read back out of a field. The text is hashed to 128 bits and the bits choose cells, so the input is gone by the time the first shape is drawn.

Accessibility and motion

  • Contrast. Body text at 4.5:1 minimum, large text at 3:1. Measured on the cream ground, --ink gives 16.9:1, --ink-body 9.3:1, --ink-muted 5.1:1 and BDSIS Red 6.7:1. The four family colours are not text-safe, as set out in §07.
  • Focus. A 3 px --is-blue-ink outline at 2 px offset, on every interactive element. Never remove it.
  • Keyboard. Drop panels open on :focus-within as well as hover, so the navigation works without a mouse.
  • Skip link. First element in the DOM, visible on focus.
  • Carousel. Must have a visible pause control, must pause on hover, and must not auto-advance under prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Motion. All transitions 180–250 ms on --ease. Everything collapses to 0.01 ms under reduced motion. That is handled globally, so do not write bespoke animation that bypasses it.
  • Language. Chinese passages inside an English page need lang="zh-Hant" so screen readers switch voice.

Two complementary dimensions

Technology advancement leads to a more interdisciplinary world with new and more complex challenges. Human needs, aspirations and the foundations of human society do not change at the same pace.

Dimension one

Interdisciplinary knowledge, skills and mindsets

Future-ready students need both broad and specialised knowledge, and the capacity to think across the boundaries of disciplines. Self-learning matters more as the half-life of any single body of knowledge shortens.

Dimension two

Deep understanding of human needs and society

Judgement rests on wisdom, experience and maturity. Those in turn rest on a deep understanding of human behaviour, needs and aspirations, and of how societies form and hold together.

The curriculum

One hundred and twenty credits minimum. Four interdisciplinary core subjects hold the scheme together; everything else you build.

Interdisciplinary core
18 credits
Self-determined curriculum
60 credits · Flexible Major
General University Requirements
27 credits
Free electives
9–12 credits

Bars are drawn to scale against the 120-credit minimum. Work-Integrated Education is recorded as training credits and sits outside this total.

CUS101 · Year 1

Introduction to Interdisciplinary Studies

Understand the world and its problems, and what interdisciplinarity means. Then choose a direction of study based on the problems you want to address. Develop a study plan. 3 credits

CUS201/301 · Years 2–3

Reflections on Contemporary Issues I & II

Student-led forums in which local, national and global issues of significance are discussed with staff and external stakeholders. Students write a reflection. 6 credits

CUS202 · Year 2

Interdisciplinary Research Methods

Research design, methodology and ethics for complex societal issues. Prepares students for self-directed study and for planning the capstone. 3 credits

CUS401 · Year 4

Interdisciplinary Capstone

A project addressing a local, national, regional or global issue of significance, delivered with inter-sector partnership. Report plus reflection. 6 credits

Three ways to build it

Every self-determined curriculum is agreed in consultation with the programme director and your academic advisor.

Pathway 01 · Solving world problems

Start without a focus and work towards one

A tailor-made study of a major local, national or global issue, such as a UNESCO Sustainable Development Goal. Subjects are selected from any department, and may include a few from other universities.

Pathway 02 · Integrating known fields

Start with a focus and build the study around it

An integrated study of two or three existing subject disciplines, with subjects selected from those majors.

Pathway 03 · Strands

Choose from one to three strands

Cultures and Societies · Sciences and Engineering · Health and Environment. Subjects are selected from the catalogue and categorised by strand.

Three layers of advising

Students confirm their focus by Semester 1 of Year 2, with support at three levels.

Layer one

Personal Academic Mentor

A renowned professor in the discipline of interest, one-on-one. Guidance on aspirations and pathway, disciplinary and interdisciplinary trends, and shared experience.

Layer two

Dean and Associate Dean

Higher-level advice grounded in personal character and aspirations, with the ability to mobilise University-wide support.

Layer three

Undergraduate Studies Support

Administrative colleagues from the College who keep academic progression smooth, and review each study plan at the end of every semester.
